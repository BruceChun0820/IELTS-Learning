## Sample

In recent years, the increasing development of science and technology has optimized many sectors such as industry, education, and public service. However, some argue that the primary goal of science should be to improve people’s living standards. I strongly agree with this view, since science can not only **enhance** individuals’ daily lives but also bring significant benefits to society **as a whole**.

To begin with, information technology is widely regarded as one of the **most visible outcomes of scientific research**. Scientific breakthroughs in computing and engineering have sparked a remarkable **digital trend**, simplifying daily processes and making a wide range of services more accessible. For example, with mobile payment systems such as PayPal, Apple Pay, and Alipay, people no longer need to worry about **carrying cash** or the **risk of theft**. This not only makes **business transactions** more efficient but also stimulates economic activity and reinforces financial security. **As a direct result of science**, information technology has clearly demonstrated the essential role of science in both society and everyday life.

Another important benefit is in the field of **healthcare**. Medical science has significantly improved people’s life **expectancy** and well-being through the development of **vaccines**, **antibiotics**, and advanced **diagnostic tools**. For instance, the discovery of **penicillin** transformed the treatment of **infectious diseases**, saving countless lives throughout the twentieth century. Moreover, innovations such as **telemedicine and AI-assisted diagnostics** have made healthcare more affordable and accessible, particularly for people living in rural or underserved areas. These examples highlight how science directly contributes to healthier and longer lives.

Admittedly, some may argue that science should also aim at expanding knowledge **for its own sake**, such as exploring outer space. Indeed, space programs have generated valuable discoveries, including satellite communication and GPS, which are now indispensable in daily life. However, these projects are justified **precisely because** they eventually enhance human living standards. In other words, even **ambitious scientific endeavors** must **be measured by** their contribution to people’s well-being, rather than by **abstract knowledge alone**.

To conclude, the most important aim of science should be to improve the quality of human life. Whether through information technology, healthcare, or even advanced space research, science achieves its greatest value only when it serves humanity. **On balance**, I firmly believe that scientific progress must always be directed toward enhancing people’s living standards.
