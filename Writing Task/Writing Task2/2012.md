## Question

*Access to clean water is a basic human right. Therefore every home should have a water supply that is provided free of charge. Do you agree or disagree?*

## Sample

In recent decades, with improvement of living standards and the advancement of techonolgy, it has become less difficult to ensure a clean water supply for most residents, and this is widely justified as a basic but essential element of daily life. Some argue that clean water should be provided free of charge to every ==household==. I do not completely agree with this view, as I believe that implementing a fully free water policy could create further social problems.

First of all, ==water treatment== and supply systems play a critical role in urban operations. They involve complex processes and require substantial funding across multiple sectors. For example, ==a lack of financial resources== may result in poor-quality ==sewage disposal== and inefficient water distribution, which could in turn raise serious concerns about water security and public health.

Moreover, making water completely free could also lead to waste and overuse. A more pratical approach is for the government to set ==a free allowance== for each household, while ==applying charges== if consumption exceeds that level. For instance, the Singaporean government has ==implemented similar policies==, which not only guarantee residents' daily water needs but also encourage a strong awareness of ==water conservation==.

Admittedly, reducing or removing water charges could indeed help citizens lower their living expenses. However, this would also increase the ==financial burden== on governments, especially in low-income regions, and might result in ==cutbacks== in other essential sectors such as education, transportation and public welfare. Therefore, I believe that water supply should not be made entirely free. 

In conclusion, access to clean water is closely liked to both individual well-being and wider social development. Lowering the cost of water would certainly benefit all residents, but making it completely free would not be a sustainable and ==sensible== policy.
