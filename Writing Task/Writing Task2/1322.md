## Essay Question

*Some people believe that nowadays we have too many choices.*

*To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement?*

*Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.*

*Write at least 250 words.*

## Sample

> In recent years, the burgeoning development of technology and science has brought us countless opportunities and innovations. Under such circumstances, some argue that modern people have too many choices. I strongly agree with this view, as technological progress and social diversity have **provided us with excessive options** in almost every aspect of life, often leading to confusion and **decision fatigue**.

> To begin with, the advancement of education and career systems has **expanded people’s choices to an unprecedented level**. Nowadays, students can select from numerous academic disciplines, online courses, and professional fields. While this diversity offers flexibility, it also makes decision-making more complicated. For instance, many young people change their majors or **career directions** several times before finding their true passion, wasting time and resources in the process. In contrast, previous generations had limited paths but clearer goals.

> Moreover, technological progress has **flooded us with** an **overwhelming number** of products, services, and information sources. In online shopping platforms, **hundreds of similar items compete for consumers’ attention**, making it difficult to determine the best choice. **Likewise**, the explosion of information on social media **exposes people to** countless opinions and lifestyles, which can easily cause anxiety and dissatisfaction. In this sense, too many options do not **necessarily enhance** our quality of life; instead, they may reduce happiness and introduce distractions.

> In conclusion, although having more choices represents progress and freedom, **the abundance of** options in modern society has also made life more complicated and stressful. Therefore, people should learn to prioritize and make **selective** decisions, rather than **being overwhelmed by the illusion of unlimited freedom**.
