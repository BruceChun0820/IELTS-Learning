## Essay Question

*In the past, everyone wore clothes according to their culture, but now people wear similar clothes all around the world. Is this a positive or a negative development? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples of your own knowledge or experience.*

## Essay Sample

In recent decades, clothing styles have become increasingly ==globalized==. Whereas people once dressed in ways that reflected their cultural traditions, it is now common to see similar ==garments== such as jeans and T-shirts worn around the world. I believe this is a negative development, as it weakens ==cultural identity== and creates harmful environmental and economic consequences.

One major concern is the loss of ==cultural heritage==. When people abandon these garments for ==standardized== global fashion, they ==risk eroding visible identity markers==.  For example, in East Asia, younger generations not only wear Western-style shirts and jeans for work or study, but also often choose Western formal suits or dresses for weddings and ceremonies instead of traditional ==attire== such as qipao or kimono. Over time, such habits reduce the daily presence of tradition, ==leaving== future generations ==less connected to== their cultural roots.

Another drawback is the environmental and economic cost created by global fashion. ==Mass-produced== clothing is often made cheaply in factories that rely on ==synthetic materials== and ==low-wage labor==. For instance, the worldwide popularity of fast-fashion brands has led to ==overproduction==, with tons of ==unsold== garments ==discarded== each year. **Such practices damage ecosystems and lock poorer regions into cycles of low-value labor rather than encouraging sustainable growth**.

Admittedly,  the trend toward similar clothing offers certain social benefits like promoting a sense of social equality. However, this ==conformity== often comes ==at the cost of individuality== leaving fewer opportunities for people to use clothing ==as a form of self-expression==. ==What appears to be== social inclusion can therefore create subtle pressures to ==conform==, limiting diversity not only across cultures but also among individuals within the same society.

To conclude, the worldwide adoption of similar clothing may simplify people's lives but it erodes cultural identity and undermines sustainability to some extent. ==On balance==, I consider this trend a negative development for societies ==in the long run==.
