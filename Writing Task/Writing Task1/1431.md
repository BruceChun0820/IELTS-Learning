The diagram illustrates **the process by which** a hydroelectric power station generates electricity. 

Overall, electricity generation in a hydroelectric station **relies on** the controlled **movement of water** between two reservoirs at different height. The same turbines and channel are used in **opposite directions** during day and night, making the process renewable and continuous.

During the daytime, water stored in high-level reservoir behind a dam is released through an open intake. As it **flows downward** through a channel, the water drives reversible turbines in the power station, which are connected to generator that converts **mechanical energy** into electricity. The produced power is then transferred via power lines to national grid for public consumption. After passing through the turbines, the water **collects** in a low level reservoir.

At night, when electricity demand is low, the system operates **in reverse**. The intake is closed, and the turbines **function as** pumps to move water from the low-level reservoir back into the high-level reservoir. This process **restores the water level** and allows the power station to be ready for electricity generation the following day, ensuring an efficient and sustainable energy cycle.
