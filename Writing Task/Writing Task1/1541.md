The pie chart shows the desitinations of anthropology graduates from one particular university after completing their undergraduate degree, while the table provides information on the salaries earned by anthropologist with five years of work experience across three employment types.

Overall, more than half of the graduates entered full-time employment, whereas a notable minority were either unemployed or engaged in postgraduate study. Regarding salaries, government employment offered the greatest proportion of top earners, while private companies **showed a stronger concentration in** middle-income levels.

In terms of graduate destinations, 52% **secured full-time jobs**, which was by far the largest group. Part-time work accounted for 15%, and 5% of all graduates combined part-time employment with postgraduate study. Meanwhile, 8% pursued full-time postgraduate education, an **idntical** proportion to those whose destinations were not known. By contrast, 12% were unemployed, which was the third-largest category after full-time job and part-time job.

Turning to salaries, 40% of freelance consultants earned over \$100,000 **annually** and another 40% were in the \$75,000-99,999 range, while only 15% and 5% **fell into** the \$50,000-74,999 and $25,000-49,999 categories **respectively**. In the government sector, half of employees earned above \$ 100,000 and **a further 30%** were in the \$75,000-99,999 range, while the remaining 20% consisted of 15% earning \$50,000-74,999 and 5% earning \$25,000-49,999. By contrast, employees in private companies were less represented in the **high-income brackets**, with 30% earning more than \$100,000 and 25% earning \$ 50,000-74,999, while another 35% received \$25,000-49,999 and a small minority of 10% were in the lowest income range.
